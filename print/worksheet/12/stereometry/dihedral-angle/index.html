Explain

Finding the angle, step by step

The task

SABCD is a regular pyramid. Its base is a square with side a=4a = 4a=4 and its height is SO=3SO = 3SO=3.

Find the angle between the side face SBC and the base. Both planes are shaded.

Find the common edge

The two planes meet along the edge BC.

The angle between two planes is measured at one point of their common edge, between two lines that are both perpendicular to that edge, one in each plane.

Perpendicular in the base

Take M, the midpoint of BC. O is the centre of the square, so OM is parallel to AB and OM⊥BCOM \perp BCOM⊥BC.

OM=a2=2OM = \dfrac{a}{2} = 2OM=2a​=2.

Perpendicular in the side face

Triangle SBC is isosceles (SB=SCSB = SCSB=SC), so its median SM is also its height: SM⊥BCSM \perp BCSM⊥BC.

Now both lines are perpendicular to BC at the same point M.

The angle

So φ=∠SMO\varphi = \angle SMOφ=∠SMO is the angle between the planes. Triangle SOM has a right angle at O:

tan⁡φ=SOOM=32,φ≈56.3∘.\tan\varphi = \frac{SO}{OM} = \frac{3}{2}, \qquad \varphi \approx 56.3^\circ .tanφ=OMSO​=23​,φ≈56.3∘.

Press Look along BC under the figure: when B and C coincide, you see the angle at its true size.

Summary

The method

To find the angle between two planes:

  1. Find their common edge.
  2. Pick one point on it. In a regular pyramid, take the midpoint M of a base edge.
  3. Draw a perpendicular to the edge in each plane: OM in the base, SM in the face.
  4. The angle between these two lines is the angle between the planes. Solve the right triangle SOM.

Do not confuse it with the angle between an edge and the base: that one is ∠SBO\angle SBO∠SBO, between SB and its projection OB.

Teacher notes

For the teacher

Pupils can say: “I can find the angle between a side face and the base of a regular pyramid, and I can tell it apart from the angle between a lateral edge and the base.”

Common mistakes and what helps

  • Choosing ∠SBO\angle SBO∠SBO (edge and base) instead of ∠SMO\angle SMO∠SMO (face and base): ask “is it a line or a plane that meets the base?”
  • Taking an angle inside one face, such as ∠SBC\angle SBC∠SBC.
  • Using OBOBOB instead of OMOMOM in triangle SOM.
  • Trusting the drawing: in the exam view the angle does not appear at its true size. “Look along BC” shows why.
