Hook

Two gardens

A rectangular garden is 3 m longer than it is wide. Its area is 40 m².

Number entryLevel 1 · threshold

How wide is the garden?

5⋅8=405 \cdot 8 = 405⋅8=40. This one can be found by trying whole numbers.
If the width is xxx metres, the length is x+3x + 3x+3 metres.
So x(x+3)=40x(x+3) = 40x(x+3)=40. Try x=4x = 4x=4, x=5x = 5x=5, …
Answer: 5

Now a second garden, also 3 m longer than it is wide, has area 30 m².

Number entryLevel 3 · basic

Getting there by trial and error takes many steps. After this lesson it takes one line.

Explain

Why the formula works

A square and a strip

Take x2+6xx^2 + 6xx2+6x. Draw x2x^2x2 as a square with side xxx, and 6x6x6x as a strip that is 6 wide and xxx long.

Split the strip

Cut the strip into two strips, each 3×x3 \times x3×x. Put one beside the square and one below it. The area is still x2+6xx^2 + 6xx2+6x.

Complete the square

One 3×33 \times 33×3 corner is missing. Adding 9 completes a square with side x+3x + 3x+3:

x2+6x+9=(x+3)2.x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2.x2+6x+9=(x+3)2.

The picture shows the case x>0x > 0x>0; the identity holds for every real xxx.

Adding 9 to both sides of x2+6x=7x^2 + 6x = 7x2+6x=7 gives (x+3)2=16(x+3)^2 = 16(x+3)2=16, so x+3=±4x + 3 = \pm 4x+3=±4: x=1x = 1x=1 or x=−7x = -7x=−7.

Exam task

Two consecutive integers

Full solutionLevel 3 · basicPUPP style · 4 points

The sum of the squares of two consecutive positive integers is 181. Find the integers.

Call the smaller integer nnn. What is the other one?
n2+(n+1)2=181n^2 + (n + 1)^2 = 181n2+(n+1)2=181.
Expand and simplify: 2n2+2n−180=02n^2 + 2n - 180 = 02n2+2n−180=0, so n2+n−90=0n^2 + n - 90 = 0n2+n−90=0.
Marking scheme: tick what your solution has
  1. Writes an equation, e.g. n2+(n+1)2=181n^2 + (n+1)^2 = 181n2+(n+1)2=181.
  2. Brings it to the form n2+n−90=0n^2 + n - 90 = 0n2+n−90=0.
  3. Solves it: n=9n = 9n=9 or n=−10n = -10n=−10.
  4. Rejects n=−10n = -10n=−10 (not positive) and answers: 9 and 10.
Worked solution

Let the integers be nnn and n+1n + 1n+1. Then n2+(n+1)2=181n^2 + (n+1)^2 = 181n2+(n+1)2=181, so 2n2+2n+1=1812n^2 + 2n + 1 = 1812n2+2n+1=181 and n2+n−90=0n^2 + n - 90 = 0n2+n−90=0.

D=1+360=361=192D = 1 + 360 = 361 = 19^2D=1+360=361=192, so n=−1±192n = \dfrac{-1 \pm 19}{2}n=2−1±19​: n=9n = 9n=9 or n=−10n = -10n=−10.

The integers are positive, so n=9n = 9n=9. Answer: 9 and 10. Check: 81+100=18181 + 100 = 18181+100=181.

Summary

What to remember

  • Write the equation as ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 first, then read aaa, bbb, ccc with their signs.
  • The discriminant D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac tells you how many roots there are:
    • D>0D > 0D>0: two roots;
    • D=0D = 0D=0: one root, x=−b2ax = -\frac{b}{2a}x=−2ab​;
    • D<0D < 0D<0: no real roots.
  • The roots are x1,2=−b±D2ax_{1,2} = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}x1,2​=2a−b±D​​. The formula comes from completing the square.
  • Check a root by substituting it. In a word problem, keep only the roots that make sense.
Teacher notes

For the teacher

Pupils can say: “I can solve any quadratic equation with the formula, explain where the formula comes from, and tell how many roots an equation has before solving it.”

Common mistakes and what helps

  • Sign errors in −4ac-4ac−4ac when aaa or ccc is negative: have pupils write D=b2−4⋅a⋅cD = b^2 - 4 \cdot a \cdot cD=b2−4⋅a⋅c with brackets around every coefficient.
  • Forgetting the ±\pm±, or dividing only D\sqrt{D}D​ by 2a2a2a: write the whole numerator over 2a2a2a first, then simplify.
  • Reading a,b,ca, b, ca,b,c before moving everything to one side (the garden: x(x+3)=30x(x+3) = 30x(x+3)=30).
  • Reading D<0D < 0D<0 as “the roots are negative”.
